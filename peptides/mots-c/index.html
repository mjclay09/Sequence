MOTS-c, simplified.

Also called Mitochondrial peptide

Early research

A peptide made by your mitochondria, the power plants in your cells, that acts a bit like exercise in a molecule.

Size

How it is taken
Injection
Status
Research compound, under FDA review

What is it?

Most of your DNA sits in the cell’s nucleus, but mitochondria have their own. MOTS-c is coded there and helps cells handle sugar and energy.

What studies show

  • In animalsIn mice, it improved insulin sensitivity, prevented diet-related weight gain and boosted exercise capacity.
  • In peoplePeople’s natural levels rise with exercise and fall with age. Direct trials in people are very early.

What we don’t know yet

  • Whether taking it does anything in people.

Side effects and cautions

  • Very little human safety data.

Is it legal?

Not FDA-approved. Reviewed by an FDA advisory committee in July 2026 for obesity and osteoporosis.
