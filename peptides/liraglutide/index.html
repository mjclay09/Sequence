What is it?

Liraglutide copies GLP-1, the gut hormone that signals fullness and slows digestion. A fatty acid attached to it makes it last about a day, so it is taken once daily rather than weekly.

Official prescribing information

Saxenda and Victoza are FDA-approved. The official label shows how it was dosed in the trials that led to approval, along with warnings and who should not use it.

Doses on the label are for the approved product under a prescriber’s care. Talk with your doctor before starting anything.

What studies show

  • In peopleIn a 56-week trial of about 3,700 adults, people lost about 8% of their body weight on average, compared with about 2.6% on placebo.
  • In peopleIn people with type 2 diabetes and heart disease, it lowered the risk of heart attack, stroke and heart-related death.
  • In peopleIn head-to-head trials, weekly semaglutide produced roughly twice the weight loss.

What we don’t know yet

  • How it compares long term with newer weekly options for weight maintenance.

Side effects and cautions

  • Nausea, vomiting and diarrhea are common early on.
  • Rare risks include pancreatitis and gallbladder problems.
  • Same thyroid cancer warning as other GLP-1 drugs.

Is it legal?

FDA-approved. Available only with a prescription. Generic versions now exist.
