Peptides Simplified/Cagrilintide

Weight loss

Cagrilintide, simplified.

Also called Long-acting amylin analog; half of CagriSema

Human studies

A lab-made copy of amylin, a fullness hormone released with insulin. It is being tested alone and paired with semaglutide as "CagriSema".

Size
37 amino acids + fatty acid
How it is taken
Weekly injection (in trials)
Status
Investigational, not approved
Available sizes
Vials: 5 mg, 10 mg

What is it?

Your pancreas releases amylin alongside insulin after meals. It tells the brain you are full and slows the stomach. Cagrilintide is a long-acting version, and it works on a different signal than GLP-1, which is why it is being combined with semaglutide.

What studies show

  • In peopleIn a 26-week trial, the top dose on its own led to about 10.8% weight loss, compared with about 3% on placebo.
  • In peopleCombined with semaglutide (CagriSema), people in a 68-week trial lost about 20% of their body weight on average.

What we don’t know yet

  • Long-term safety, since it is still in trials.
  • Whether it will be approved on its own or only as part of CagriSema.

Side effects and cautions

  • Nausea and other stomach side effects, similar to GLP-1 drugs.
  • Research-grade products are not the tested drug and have no quality control.

Is it legal?

Not FDA-approved as of our last review. Available only inside clinical trials.
