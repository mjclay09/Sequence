Semaglutide, simplified.

Also called Ozempic, Wegovy, Rybelsus

A lab-made copy of a gut hormone called GLP-1 that tells your brain you are full. It is the active ingredient in Ozempic and Wegovy.

What is it?

After you eat, your gut releases a hormone called GLP-1. It tells your brain you have had enough and slows how fast your stomach empties. Semaglutide acts like GLP-1 but lasts about a week instead of a few minutes.

What studies show

  • In peopleIn a large 68-week trial, adults with obesity lost about 15% of their body weight on average, compared with about 2% on placebo.
  • In peopleIn a trial of 17,000+ people with heart disease and excess weight, it lowered the risk of heart attack, stroke and heart-related death by about 20%.
  • In peopleIt lowers blood sugar in type 2 diabetes, which is what it was first approved for.

What we don’t know yet

  • Most people regain much of the weight after stopping.
  • Very long-term effects past a decade are still being studied.

Side effects and cautions

  • Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea and constipation are common, especially early on.
  • Rare risks include pancreatitis and gallbladder problems.
  • Not for people with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2.

Is it legal?

FDA-approved. Available only with a prescription.
